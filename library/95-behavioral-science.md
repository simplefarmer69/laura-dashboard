# Behavioral science of crypto participation

Operator directive 2026-09-13: LAURA studies how humans decide in crypto and applies it
to grow StonkBrokers. This page is the working model; Scholar's standing study refreshes
it through the notebook, and the narrative, growth, community and BD agents act on it.

## The boundary first

Influence in LAURA's hands is **honest persuasion**: making true things easier to notice,
understand and act on. The charter forbids the other kind and code enforces it: no
sockpuppets, no manufactured urgency, no invented scarcity, no return promises, no
price targets, no wash volume. Anything below that would also fail on its own terms:
crypto crowds punish detected manipulation harder than they reward it.

## What actually moves people (evidence-backed, in rough order of strength)

1. **Social proof, made visible.** People enter when they see others like them
   entering. Real numbers do the work: holders, trades in the last hour, a named wallet
   acting. Show the crowd, never fake it. Live counts beat adjectives.
2. **Narrative and identity.** Meme stocks proved that a shared story ("apes together",
   "diamond hands") turns a trade into a membership. Tokens win when holding them says
   something about the holder. Every launch must carry a message a holder would repeat.
3. **Loss aversion and regret.** Fear of missing a move is stronger than desire for the
   move. Ethical use: state real windows (a fair-start tax decaying on a clock, a
   graduation that locks LP) plainly and early, so nobody learns them afterwards.
   Unethical use (fake countdowns, "last chance") is banned.
4. **Salience and cadence.** Attention goes to what shows up reliably. A rhythm (daily
   grade, regular launches, the Cafe Bar) trains the audience to look. Silence is the
   enemy, repetition of the same line the second enemy.
5. **Transparency as trust.** An agent that shows its wallet, its caps and its failures
   earns a credibility discount no marketing buys. Post the ledger, including misses.
6. **Reciprocity and small commitments.** People who did one tiny thing (replied,
   claimed, voted) do the next. Ask for small, reversible actions first.
7. **Anchoring and framing.** The first number people see frames all the others.
   Lead with the verifiable number that matters (protocol revenue, holders), never with
   a hypothetical.
8. **Authority and proximity.** Attention flows from accounts people already trust.
   Replying with substance to a Robinhood employee or a KOL borrows their audience for
   a moment; earn it with information, never with flattery.
9. **Novelty with a familiar frame.** New things get looked at; familiar things get
   trusted. A launch that riffs on a live narrative with an original name hits both.
10. **Friction.** Every extra click halves participation. Link the exact page, quote the
    exact contract, give the exact command.

## Where the evidence comes from

Behavioral finance (Kahneman and Tversky on prospect theory; Thaler on nudges; Shiller
on narrative economics), the meme-stock literature since 2021, exchange growth
playbooks, and the running study Scholar maintains in the notebook under
"behavioral". Cite sources when quoting a finding; browse for the primary text.

## How each agent uses this

- **Quill / Momentum (X):** one true number, one human frame, one exact link. Reply
  to people, not to the void. Cadence over volume.
- **Steward (community):** rituals (a daily line, a weekly recap), small asks, public
  acknowledgement of members who act.
- **Broker (BD):** partners respond to proof of a live crowd and a clear next step.
- **Mint (launches):** every token is a message; the tax schedule is part of the story.
- **Nudge (behavioral analyst):** turns findings into tested tactics and grades them.
