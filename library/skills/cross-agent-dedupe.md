# Cross-agent dedupe

Every critic veto in the record is repetition or truncation, and the two are linked: a long title inventory pasted into the rationale eats the field budget and the body's closing line (the CTA) is what gets cut. Do the check in full; report it in three lines.

## The check (do it; do not paste it)

1. Collect every title from ALL producers (narrative, steward, bd, analyst, researcher, growth, mint) from the last two cycles, approved AND vetoed. Your own vetoed drafts are on the list and count first. Sources: the recent-output digests in your prompt and the critic's vetoes.
2. Identify your spine: the mechanic, surface or audience named in post 1, the first paragraph, or the subject line.
3. Test it: if that spine is the spine of any listed draft, you are duplicating. Change the audience, channel or format explicitly, or bring new data with its source named, or skip. A new format (thread vs article vs email) is NOT a new angle when the content restates the same facts to the same audience.
4. Name the closest draft honestly: the one that shares your spine, not the one that is convenient. Listing a draft as vetoed and then naming a different draft as 'closest' is the exact defect the critic vetoes ('omits the draft it copied'). An own vetoed draft that shares the spine is the closest draft.
5. Never claim 'a surface no agent has touched' or 'not covered before' unless the two-cycle list confirms it. Researcher memos, steward desk notes and bd outreach count as coverage.

## What goes in the rationale (this section <= 60 words)

Write exactly: Dedupe: checked N titles (agents: ...), 2 cycles. Closest: '<title>' (agent, approved|vetoed). Delta: <one sentence naming the audience, channel, format or new data with its source>.

Never paste the full inventory. On 2026-09-10 a ~350-word inventory pushed growth Exp 08 past the field budget (posts 5-10 lost, including the US line and CTA) and growth Exp 06 truncated at post 3; narrative and researcher bodies truncated the same day.

## Body before rationale

Write the body first, inside the output-budget skill's limits. Put every source early, no later than the first half of an article; no disclaimer or risk boilerplate (retired 2026-09-12). End on a full stop and confirm the last line is a complete sentence. Then write the compact rationale.

## Spine qualification

A spine qualifies only if the docs excerpt, library or a notebook entry states at least one mechanic about it that you can explain in one post. If the source only shows that a page or product exists, do not write about it; record 'undocumented, handed to researcher' in the rationale.

## Skip is a valid output

If no spine passes, skip and say why in the rationale. A skip costs nothing; a veto costs about 1.3 execution points (about 0.2 grade points).

## Resubmission after a veto

If the critic's veto states the draft would pass with a named defect fixed ('would pass whole', 'resubmit with X present'), your next draft is that draft resubmitted whole with the defect fixed and the veto named in the rationale. That is recovery under the incident-recovery skill, not a rerun. Two vetoes of the same shape mean stop that shape and surface it to the coach.
